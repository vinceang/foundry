# Sluice backlog

The build queue for **Sluice**, Foundry's duration phase. Foundry's own queue is
`docs/foundry-backlog.md`; the two are kept apart because they screen subjects on
different rules and mixing them means filtering every entry twice.

**The screening rule.** Foundry takes subjects whose value is an **object**.
Sluice takes subjects whose value is **duration** — a process, a route, a shift,
a cycle. The test before an entry goes in:

> **If you removed the passage of time, would the subject survive?**

If yes, it belongs in the Foundry queue. A subject that only becomes interesting
because we animated it is a Foundry site with animation stuck on, which is the
exact failure this phase exists to avoid.

Every entry must name its **duration** — the real span, with real units — and a
**signature interaction** that is an instrument rather than a diagram. If the
interaction could be replaced by a caption without loss, it is not one.

See `docs/sluice-standard.md` for the grammar rule (scroll is a controller, never
a trigger), the never-a-blank-page policy, and the review requirements. Clear
`docs/foundry-fingerprints.md` before writing markup — Sluice rows are gated
against Foundry rows and the reverse, since both phases share tools, taste and a
showcase.

## Building

### Guérande saltern — the first SLUICE site

- **Light:** bright — midday glare on open water, deliberately not Nocturne. The collection is too dark and this subject has no night in it.
- **Place idea:** the *marais salants* of Guérande, Loire-Atlantique, Brittany — a thousand-year-old clay-floored salt marsh worked by *paludiers* with a wooden *las*, producing *gros sel* and *fleur de sel* by evaporation alone. No pumps, no heat, no machinery.
- **Why it earns a place:** the photography is aerial geometry — hundreds of shallow clay pans in a hand-cut grid, water shifting green to rose to white as it concentrates, and salt heaped in blinding cones on the banks. Nothing in the collection is bright, aerial, or geometric, and nothing else is a landscape that is also a machine. The audience is chefs and specialist grocers.
- **The duration:** seawater takes about three weeks to cross the cascade, climbing from roughly 30 g/L to 260 g/L across a sequence of named ponds. Crystallisation happens in a window of a few hours, on hot still afternoons only, and one shower of rain resets weeks of work.
- **Signature interaction:** **the cascade.** Scroll drives seawater through the pond sequence — *vasière*, *cobier*, *fares*, *adernes*, *œillets* — and the water's salinity, colour and depth change continuously with position. Fully reversible: scrub back and the brine dilutes. At the last pond, hold and the *fleur de sel* forms on the surface; a weather control alongside lets the visitor put a day of rain into the season and watch the cascade reset.
- **Other page ideas:** the *œillet* as a unit of production, with one paludier's named pans and what each yielded this season; the *las* and why the tool has not changed; *gros sel* versus *fleur de sel* argued by where in the pan each forms; an order form for the season's harvest, since salt sells well under $5,000 and a letter would be absurd.
- **Notes:** display face must not be a serif — 23 of 25 sites are serif and Fraunces / EB Garamond / Faustina are spent. People throughout with faces, sunburnt and bent over the rake, not one distant figure.

## Proposed — awaiting approval

### Tide mill — Brittany

- **Light:** grey-green Atlantic, wet granite, low cloud. Neither the collection's warm dark nor open daylight.
- **Place idea:** a working tide mill on the Rance estuary or the Île de Bréhat — a pond that fills at high water, a gate that shuts, and a wheel that turns only while the sea drops.
- **Why it earns a place:** the strongest instrument on this list. A tide curve is a controller by nature, and the whole business depends on reading it. Also the phase's namesake mechanism, which is either a statement of intent or too cute — decide before building.
- **The duration:** the mill grinds only on the falling tide, roughly five hours per window, twice a day, each window arriving about **50 minutes later than yesterday**. The miller's working life runs on the moon, not the clock — some weeks he starts at 3am.
- **Signature interaction:** scrub the tide. Pond fills, gate closes, head-height builds, the wheel turns, the window opens and shuts. Then pull back and watch a month of working hours slide later and later across the calendar — the thing a caption cannot give you is the feeling of the schedule slipping.
- **Notes:** the argument is one line — *the mill runs when the water falls*. Human presence is a miller working nights because the water said so.

### Anagama kiln firing — Japan or Wales

- **Light:** night, fire, faces lit orange. Dark, but **earned** dark, which is the distinction the register question now tests.
- **Place idea:** a single-chamber climbing kiln fired once or twice a year, with a crew feeding it in four-hour shifts.
- **Why it earns a place:** the duration *is* the product — the ash glaze does not exist until days of flame have deposited it. Also the best answer on this list to the faces problem: a crew at 3am is unposed human presence.
- **The duration:** four to six days of continuous burn, someone awake at every hour, several cords of pine, temperature climbing to around 1300°C and held.
- **Signature interaction:** scrub the firing curve. Temperature climbs, pyrometric cones bend in sequence, ash accumulates on the pot's shoulder, and the flame path marks one side and not the other. The pot is in a genuinely different condition at each scroll position.
- **Notes:** hold this one back until the collection is less dark overall. Right site, wrong moment to open a phase with.

### Wheelbuilder — Surrey or the South Downs

- **Light:** workshop daylight, north window.
- **Place idea:** a wheelbuilder rather than a framebuilder. **Traccia already owns custom steel frames and a geometry configurator** — a second framebuilder collides on 5 of 6 fingerprint dimensions and must not be built.
- **Why it earns a place:** it clears the gate precisely because it is about time rather than geometry. A wheel is not built, it is brought up.
- **The duration:** tension comes up over successive passes around the rim, never in one; then the wheel settles over its first few hundred miles and is trued again.
- **Signature interaction:** scrub the build. Spoke tension rises pass by pass around the rim as a live tension map, the rim pulls into true, and the last stretch of the scroll is the first 300 miles — the wheel relaxing and going slightly out again.
- **Notes:** this is the Surrey bike-shop idea, reframed. The restoration-and-respray angle and the thirty-year service-life angle are alternates on the same subject; pick one, not two.

### Vanilla curing — Réunion or Madagascar

- **Light:** hot, humid, high sun and dark drying sheds alternating.
- **Place idea:** a curing house where green pods arrive with no flavour at all and leave six months later as vanilla.
- **The duration:** killing, sweating, then a daily cycle of sun and blanket for weeks, then months of conditioning in closed boxes. Vanillin does not exist in the green pod; it is manufactured entirely by time.
- **Signature interaction:** scrub the cure. Pods darken, shrink, gain frost, and a live moisture and vanillin readout climbs. The daily sun-and-sweat cycle is a fast oscillation inside a slow six-month curve, which reads beautifully as a controller.
- **Notes:** the strongest "the product does not exist without duration" case on the list.

### Affinage cellar — Jura or Aveyron

- **Light:** cold cellar, low and even, stone.
- **Place idea:** a cave-ageing house where wheels are turned, brushed and salted on a schedule for months to years.
- **The duration:** four months to three years per wheel, each one turned and rubbed on a named interval, with the rind built entirely by that repetition.
- **Signature interaction:** scrub a wheel's ageing. The rind builds, the paste changes, the weight falls, and the tally of turns and brushings accumulates against the calendar.
- **Notes:** risk of feeling like a diagram rather than an instrument. Needs a real decision surfaced to the visitor — when to pull a wheel — or it is a caption with a slider.

### Transhumance — Pyrenees or the Carpathians

- **Light:** high altitude, hard sun, long shadows.
- **Place idea:** a family that walks its herd up in June and down in September, the same route their grandparents walked.
- **The duration:** a route and a cycle at once — days of walking, a season on the high pasture, then back down.
- **Signature interaction:** scrub the route. Altitude, temperature, grass and distance-from-home all change with scroll position, and the herd's milk changes with the pasture.
- **Notes:** the only route subject here. Watch the collision risk with Velum's flight-path trace — the shape of "a line with a moving position on it" is already taken, so this needs a different form.
