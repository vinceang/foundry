# Brief — Aubade Hotel Two: architectural and composed

*Direction locked 2026-07-16.*

This is the contemporary counterpart to Hotel One and the second project in
Foundry’s Aubade series. It shares the daylight doctrine in
[`aubade-taste-profile.md`](aubade-taste-profile.md), but its identity comes
from architecture rather than domestic collection.

## The intent

Create a polished, contemporary boutique hotel where hospitality is expressed
through proportion, orientation, material precision, and silence. It must not
read as a tidier or more expensive version of Hotel One.

Hotel One welcomes the guest into a life. **Hotel Two gives the guest a new
way to see space.**

Working proposition:

> A hotel shaped by light, proportion, and silence.

## Concrete working subject

Until the location and name are chosen, design toward a **35–50 room
architecture-led hotel in an urban or coastal setting**. The building has deep
window reveals, planted courts, a mineral pool, and rooms classified partly by
orientation and quality of light.

Audience: a design-aware traveler who values architecture, calm service, and
the experience of a place over domestic familiarity.

| Bronze oak | `#6b5c3b` | Joinery, grounded controls, depth |

Black or charcoal window frames remain important but are not yet assigned a

contrast independently of the five image-derived swatches.

## Material and spatial language

- Mineral plaster, limestone or pale concrete
- Deep window reveals and crisp shadow lines
- Glass, blackened bronze, or brushed metal used as structure
- White oak or walnut with precise joinery
- Contemporary art at architectural scale
- Greenery integrated as courts and views rather than repeated potted props
- Fewer objects than Hotel One; each carries greater visual weight

The project may introduce one location-specific clear color—mineral blue,
oxidized green, saffron, or clay—only after the place makes that color
inevitable. Do not add terracotta by reflex.

## Page thesis and anatomy

The hero should reveal the building’s governing proportion: a court, facade,
window bay, stair, or long wall cut by light. Copy occupies structural negative
space rather than floating in a card.

Working page arc:

`The building → Rooms by light → Water / bathing → The table → Court / garden → Place → Stay / availability → Correspondence`

Room presentation can become more systematic than Hotel One, but must remain
editorial before it becomes comparative. Use orientation, view, floor, and
light—not marketing tiers—as the primary distinctions.

## Signature interaction — orientation and light

Do not repeat Hotel One’s timeline through a guest’s day. Hotel Two’s
signature is architectural orientation: an understated plan, compass, or
section reveals how light enters each room.

Possible vocabulary:

```text
EAST   First light / garden court
SOUTH  High light / pool
WEST   Last light / city or sea
```

As a room is selected, its image, shadow direction, and concise description
change together. The interaction should teach the building, not decorate the
booking flow.

## Type and geometry

- Typography should be more composed and architectural than Hotel One’s old
  house voice. Test a contemporary serif or warm rational sans; do not assume
  Libre Caslon or Manrope carries over.
- Use a disciplined spatial grid derived from bays, windows, or floor-plan
  proportions.
- Image frames may behave like apertures or sliding planes. Avoid glassmorphism,
  floating beige cards, and generic masonry galleries.
- Keep edges exact. Softness comes from light and material, not rounded UI.

## Copy voice

Cooler than Hotel One, but never clinical. The authority comes from precise
observation:

- “West rooms receive the final two hours of light.”
- “Each floor contains six rooms and one open-air court.”
- “The pool is cut from the same limestone as the lower hall.”

Avoid architectural jargon that a guest cannot feel and luxury adjectives
that the building should prove.

## Image set required

- Exterior or facade establishing the architecture
- One hero court, stair, or long interior axis
- East-, south-, and west-facing room variations
- Pool or water chapter
- Contemporary bath
- Restaurant or breakfast room
- Planted court / garden as architecture
- Material close-ups: stone joint, hardware, joinery, linen
- One restrained human service moment for hospitality

Hotel One’s objects, olive pots, art, and furniture must not be recycled into
this set. Coherence comes from the second hotel’s building and location.

## Do not solve yet

- Final name, city, country, or coastline
- Building history: new construction versus adaptive reuse
- Exact room count and amenity program
- Final structural dark and optional clear accent
- Typefaces
- Booking model
- Whether the orientation interaction uses a real plan or an interpretive one

## First acts of the build session

1. Read this brief, `foundry-series.md`, and `aubade-taste-profile.md`.
2. Choose the exact place and decide whether the architecture is new or reused.
3. Define the building’s governing geometry and light orientations.
4. Expand the color board into 6–10 coherent architectural image references.
5. Write the site’s own `DESIGN.md` and vocabulary.
6. Build the hero and orientation interaction as the first proof; critique
   before building rooms or booking UI.
