# 包屋 TSUTSUMIYA — Design source of truth

*Written 2026-08-06, before any component. A wrapping-goods house in
Itachibori, Osaka, in the Nocturne register: the front room sells cloth; the
back room fills it.*

## The one-line brief

**This house does not make the thing. It makes the thing unseen — and that is
the harder craft.**

## Identity

**Name:** **包屋 Tsutsumiya** — "the wrapping house." The verb 包む *tsutsumu*
means to wrap; the compound 包み隠す *tsutsumi-kakusu* — literally "to wrap and
hide" — is the ordinary Japanese idiom for covering something up. The house's
front trade and its real trade are the same word. Nothing about the name is
invented: it is a plausible Edo shop name (〜屋) for a merchant in wrapping
goods, and it is also a confession.

**Series:** **Nocturne**, and the call is worth recording because the obvious
objection is Vesper — this is a world built on not being seen, which is
Vesper's stated territory.

Vesper is the register of **things in transit**: the horizon, the tarmac, the
hour between. Tsutsumiya is the opposite condition. It is a **fixed interior**
— a counter, a board, a lantern — and its whole subject is what happens to an
object *before* it travels. The handover is one paragraph at the end; the site
is the room. The native hour is the hour of the dog (戌の刻, roughly 8pm),
after the ironmongers' quarter has shuttered, when the shop is lit by one paper
andon and the wrapping is done. That is Nocturne's working condition exactly.

**The distance from Shokunin** (the site this could most easily collapse into):
Shokunin is a **maker** of Japanese artifacts under a hard point-source lamp —
lacquer, steel, gold, lacing. Tsutsumiya is a **merchant**, its materials are
cloth, hemp, washi and cinnabar, and its light comes *through paper* rather
than off metal, so nothing on the site is ever fully revealed. Shokunin's
subject is an object achieving its final form; Tsutsumiya's subject is an
object disappearing into a square of cloth. They also read nothing alike:
Shokunin sets Latin display faces with kanji rails as decoration; this site is
typeset in **mincho and gothic**, the actual Japanese book and working faces,
so the Japanese is structural rather than ornamental.

**Place:** A lane behind the wharf on the **Itachibori canal (立売堀)** in
Osaka — historically the city's iron and edged-tool wholesale district. A house
that takes delivery of forged iron there is the least remarkable thing on the
street. Two rooms: the front room of shelves and cloth, and the board behind
it. No address is given on the site; the ward is.

**Founded:** 天和二年 (Tenna 2, 1682), as a dealer in wrapping cloth, flat
woven cord, oiled paper and paulownia boxes. It still is.

**The people:**
- **The wrapper** — ties. Six knots, and the choice of which is the whole job.
- **The tester** — proves. Every edge is drawn once across a rolled mat;
  every rope is hung with eighteen *kan* (67.5 kg) for a count of one hundred.
  Nothing leaves the house untested, and the house says which pieces failed.

**Proposition:** a parcel that reads as something else.

## Money, and therefore the order form

Osaka traded in **silver by weight** (銀目 *ginme*, denominated in 匁 *monme*),
where Edo traded in gold — a real distinction and the house's own unit. Prices
are given in monme of silver, with a modern equivalent beside them.

Most of the register sits between **¥19,000 and ¥310,000** — well under the
$5,000 line — so commitment 7 makes a **real, structured order form the primary
path**, not a "write to us" gesture. Only the long blades cross it, and they
are handled separately and said to be. The form takes an order request by
mailto; it never captures a card.

**The kinari rule** (this register's answer to "one metal behaves as light"):
the light here is **paper**, not metal. Everything the house knows is written
on unbleached washi, and the page reads in it. The single *applied* colour is
**shu**, the vermilion of the seal paste, and it appears only where the house
has actually **marked** something: the seal itself, a verified provenance, the
active state in the wrap, focus rings, one primary action per view. If shu
exceeds ~5% of a frame it has stopped being a seal; pull back.

*Kachi-iro* (褐色 / 勝色) is a real, named indigo — the darkest shade in the
vat, whose name puns on 勝つ, "to win." That pun is why samurai wore it, and
it is why this house's cloth is that colour and not black.

## Type

- **Shippori Mincho B1** — the display voice. A genuine *mincho*: the face
  Japanese books and ledgers are set in, with an old-style Latin cut drawn to
  sit beside it. Statements, chapter openings, the numbers.
- **Zen Kaku Gothic New** — the working voice. A quiet Japanese gothic with a
  clean Latin: measurements in *sun* and *monme*, the register's tallies, the
  order form, labels.

One type system carries both scripts, which is the point — no sibling site has
a Japanese-native pairing (Shokunin: Marcellus/Manrope; Barchetta:
Faustina/Chivo; Sant'Agata, Nipis, Veta, Aresta, Velum all Latin-only).

## Signature geometry

**The turned corner.** A square with one corner folded over — a wrap in its
first move. It is the only ornament: the wordmark's mark, the favicon, the
chapter markers, the checked state in the wrap. Everything else is drawn
square, because a furoshiki is a square and so is a ledger's ruling. Zero
border-radius. The one curve permitted anywhere is the loop of a knot.

## The opening move — the ledger line

**Not** a tracked caps label above a heading. Each chapter opens as **a line
in the house's purchase ledger (仕入帳)**: a hairline rule in *ai*, with the
folio in kanji numerals and a real era date sitting on it, right-aligned the
way a ledger's marginalia sit — then the display statement below.

It earns its place because each date is a **fact the chapter then uses**:

| Folio | Date | What it records |
|---|---|---|
| 帳 一 | 天和二年 霜月 | the founding entry — the shop opens on cloth |
| 帳 二 | 享保九年 水無月 | the register is first ruled into columns |
| 帳 三 | 明和五年 卯月 | the Awa indigo contract — kachi-iro from Tokushima |
| 帳 四 | 文化七年 神無月 | the four sources fixed; the testing rule written |
| 帳 五 | 天保三年 長月 | the wrapper's six knots recorded |
| 帳 六 | 令和七年 皐月 | the handover terms, as they stand |

Recorded here so it is held site-wide and never drifts into decoration.

## The signature interaction — 包 "the wrap"

The one bold spend, built and critiqued first, fused with the order form per
doctrine. **The cloth decides what you may take.**

A two-pane panel. **Left:** the register of wares as steppers — each row a
coded trade name in the house's own cant, its true name beneath, and its real
numbers (length in *sun*, weight in *monme*, price in monme of silver).
**Right:** a large SVG seen from directly above — a square of *kachi-iro* cloth
on the wrapping board. As wares are added they are **packed and drawn** lying
on the cloth; then the four corners fold in and the *ma-musubi* draws tight.
Every change redraws the whole thing.

Live readouts, all computed from one model:

- **包み** the finished parcel — length × width × height in *sun* (and cm)
- **目方** weight — in *monme* (and kg)
- **布** the cloth required, by the house's stated rule:
  **cloth side ≥ 1.5 × the parcel's diagonal + 2.5 × its height + 8 sun for the
  knot** — resolving to 二幅 (68) / 三幅 (105) / 四幅 (130) cm, or nothing
- **見え** *mie* — **how the parcel reads on the street.** This is the point of
  the whole interaction. From the finished silhouette the house names what a
  passer-by would take it for:
  - **薬箱** *kusuribako* — a medicine chest (compact, boxy)
  - **反物** *tanmono* — a bolt of cloth (long, slim)
  - **荷** *ni* — a porter's load (passes, but wants two hands and a pole)
  - **通らぬ** *tōranu* — **it does not pass.** Something protrudes, or no
    cloth is wide enough.

When the verdict is 通らぬ, the house **says what to do about it**: split it
into two parcels, or take the length in the open. This is the site's best
moment and its most honest: **a long blade never passes.** A *katana* is
marked 別 — it does not travel in cloth at all, by any arrangement the form can
make. A *shikomi-zue* (the staff that isn't) is marked 手持: carried in the
hand, in the open, not in the parcel — it adds to the price and not to the
bundle. The product argues with the buyer, and the constraint is real.

**「この包みを頼む」** carries the whole spec into the order form and scrolls
to it.

**JS off / `?nofx` / reduced motion:** the SVG renders a sensible default
parcel fully drawn and knotted with every readout printed, and the controls are
real number inputs inside the form — so the order still submits the chosen
spec. All reveal animation is gated behind an `html.js` class set by an inline
head script, so a JS-off visitor gets the finished composition rather than
content stranded at opacity 0.

## How the site takes an order

**渡し Watashi** collects a real commission:

- **包み** — the parcel, pre-filled from the wrap and shown as its ledger line
- **名** — the name you are known by *(the house does not ask for the other one)*
- **口利き** — who spoke for you **(required; the referral gate, honestly
  enforced, with the reason given)**
- **渡し場** — the ward for the handover (立売堀 / 船場 / 道頓堀 / 天満 /
  堺筋 / elsewhere — say where)
- **刻** — the hour, in the old reckoning: 暮六つ (≈18:00) / 五つ (≈20:00) /
  四つ (≈22:00) / 九つ (≈24:00)
- **便り** — where the reply goes (email)

Hands off by **mailto** with `enctype="text/plain"` so every field lands
legibly as an order request. Every state designed: rest, focus, filled, error
(named in shu beside the field, in the house's voice), submitting, and a
ceremonial 受 *uke* — "received" — confirmation stating what was taken down and
when the reply comes.

## Imagery — the Tsutsumiya recipe

**One recipe: light through paper.** A single *andon* — a lantern whose light
passes through washi — so the subject is generously and fully modelled but the
edges stay soft and nothing is ever quite fully revealed. Nocturne band, target
mean luma 20–45: rich, never murky. That "half-seen" quality is the
differentiator from Shokunin's hard point-source lamp, and it is thematic —
this is a house that does not show you the whole object.

Constant batch phrase (identical across every plate): *"lit by a single paper
andon lantern, its light passing through washi so the subject is softly but
generously and fully modelled, warm ivory key with deep indigo-black
surroundings, unbleached hemp and deepest indigo textiles, museum documentary
photography on a large-format camera, shallow depth of field, rich and legible
rather than murky, no text, no lettering, no characters, no logos."*

Shot list: `mise` (the front room), `nuno` (the cloth, macro), `cho` (the
ledger, brush and seal), `kanagu` (iron field tools), `tekagi` (climbing
claws), `shozoku` (the indigo work suit), `tabi` (split-toe boots), `nawa`
(rope and grapple), `te-musubi` (**human** — hands drawing the knot), `te-shiken`
(**human** — the tester's face lit by the work), `tsuchi` (the tang and the
punch mark, macro), `watashi` (a parcel on a stone step by the canal).

Wares are photographed as **museum artifacts with provenance**, never as
threat: no gore, no menace, no use depicted. The gravity is a sword school's,
applied to a tradesman's stock.

### Video — a deliberate deviation, recorded

The Nocturne profile caps video at one per page. This build carries **three**,
at Vince's explicit direction for immersion, and each is held to the profile's
actual test — *would CSS fake this?*:

1. **mise** — the hero. The andon breathes, smoke drifts, the hanging cloth
   barely stirs. Ambient life CSS cannot fake.
2. **te-musubi** — the knot drawn tight. **Revealed geometry**, which is
   precisely what the profile says video exists for, and it is the site's
   entire craft in four seconds.
3. **watashi** — lantern light moving on canal water behind a parcel that never
   moves. Light raking a surface.

Each is generated from the exact still it layers over (`start_image` = that
still's job) so the loop can dip back to the image seamlessly; desktop-only,
lazy, `muted playsinline loop`, poster = the still, and hidden entirely under
`prefers-reduced-motion` and `?nofx`.

## Never

Ninja costume, masks, shadowy figures, throwing poses, blood, menace · a
"stealth/assassin" register of any kind · pure black · a second applied colour
· a uniform card grid for the register · rounded corners · a tracked caps label
above every heading · legible generated Japanese in a photograph (models write
nonsense; every plate is prompted textless) · "ancient secrets," "the way of
the shadow," or any mystical-Japan voice · a "write to us" gesture in place of
the order form · murky underexposed plates.

## Copy voice

A merchant's, not a mystic's. Flat, exact, faintly dry. Weights, lengths,
hours, wards, counts, failures. *"Everything this house sells is a container.
What the container holds is a separate ledger, and it is not on the shelf."*
*"A long blade does not travel in cloth. It travels as a staff, or it travels
in pieces, or it does not travel."* The word "secret" appears nowhere; the
secrecy is entirely in what the copy declines to say.

### Amendment — the register gets its plates (same day)

The ledger originally ran as pure text, on the backlog's line that *"the
catalog format is itself the drama — a printed tradesman's register, not a
storefront."* That is right about **form** and wrong as an excuse to withhold
evidence: seven of thirteen wares had a plate somewhere on the page and six had
none, which was an accident of what got generated rather than a decision. And
the signature's control column was thirteen rows of pure text — you were being
asked to choose what goes in a parcel with no idea what any of it looked like.

The house's own character settles it. A shop that hangs every rope with
eighteen *kan* and publishes its failure rate keeps a specimen plate against
each line. **The parcel conceals; the book does not.**

So every ware now carries a square **accession plate** — 96px in the 帳 rows,
56px in the 包 control rows. This is deliberately *not* the thing the Nocturne
profile bans: a uniform card grid reads as inventory, but a plate at the left
of a ruled row is museum accession language, and the ledger stays a list.

Nine plates were shot square for the purpose (`w-*`); four reuse a chapter
plate. Two things had to be fixed by measurement rather than eye:

- **The generator frames "tightly framed" loosely** — at 96px the ware was
  ~25px inside a field of hemp. Sources are centre-cropped before optimizing,
  with an explicit per-ware centre in `crop-wares.mjs`, because a naive centre
  crop of a tall chapter portrait lands on the lantern rather than the ware.
- **The first layout put the cant *below* its plate**, which grew the row to
  ~240px and made the kanji read as belonging to the row beneath it. The
  accession cell is now a two-column grid — plate, then cant and gloss beside
  it — so the row stays short and the reading order is name, then specimen.

Specimens are ≤ 84KB each (13 wares × 2 widths). The katana's first take came
back letterboxed with white bands and was re-shot. Mobile register rows now run
single-column so the entry reads top to bottom.


### 控 — the accession entry (commitment 10)

Every plate in the 帳 register opens into **the record the row had no room
for**: the plate at 1200px, the true name, the entry line, and then the five
things the ledger row could not carry — 寸法 in *sun* and centimetres, 目方 in
*monme* and grams, 銀 in monme of silver and yen, 出処 the source house, and 験
the test that piece is put through. It closes with what the cloth does about
it: goes in the cloth / carried in the hand / the house does not carry it.

This is the point of the pattern and the reason it is now doctrine. An
enlargement that is only *larger* has not earned the interaction; this one is
where a buyer learns that the 夜縄 comes from Iga-Ueno and is hung at eighteen
*kan* before it leaves. The 験 line is read from `SOURCES`, so the register
chapter and the dialog can never disagree about what was tested.

Built as a native `<dialog>`, which gives the focus trap, Escape, and modal
semantics without hand-rolling them. Verified behaviourally rather than by
inspection: opens on Enter from a keyboard-focused plate, focus moves into the
dialog, Escape closes it, **focus returns to the exact trigger**, the backdrop
closes it, and the body scroll lock is applied and released.

Two details worth keeping:

- **The trigger is a real `<a href>` to the full image**, so a JS-off visitor
  still reaches the plate. The dialog only ever upgrades it.
- **The plate is inserted by script rather than shipped as an empty tag.** The
  anti-pattern detector was right to flag a src-less image as a broken-image
  box; the fix is an empty `<figure>` that script fills. *(It then flagged the
  literal string in the code comment explaining this — the one false positive
  of the build, resolved by rewording the comment.)*
