# BARCHETTA — Design source of truth

*Written 2026-07-31, before any component. A Neapolitan bespoke tailor in the
Nocturne register: a first-floor sartoria off Piazza dei Martiri.*

## The one-line brief

**A Neapolitan coat is an argument about what to leave out — and you can only
prove an absence by opening the coat.**

## Identity

**Name:** **Barchetta** — the *taschino a barchetta*, the little-boat breast
pocket, is the one detail no other tailoring tradition cuts: a shallow
crescent that rises toward the shoulder instead of sitting square. It is the
house's own signature and, in a city built around a bay, its name. The word is
real tailoring vocabulary, not decoration.

**Series:** **Nocturne** — and this is the call worth recording, because the
backlog proposed Vesper and the obvious objection is that Neapolitan tailoring
is *about lightness*.

The subject of this site is not the finished coat in Mediterranean sun. It is
the coat's **interior** — floating canvas, haircloth chest piece, the
pick-stitch, the gathered shoulder — which exists only on the tailor's board,
under one lamp, and which the wearer will never see again once it is closed.
Nocturne's doctrine is *craft under one lamp, the artifact emerging from
darkness*, and that is literally the working condition here: the *giacchettaie*
finish by lamp, and a coat is opened and shown to a client across a lit board
in a dark room. Vesper is for things in transit; nothing here is in transit.

The Naples daylight is not banished — it is the one warm window at the end of
the page, which is exactly where the finished coat goes.

**Place:** A first-floor sartoria on **Vico Santa Maria a Cappella Vecchia**,
the lane running off Piazza dei Martiri toward the Chiaia seafront. Two rooms:
the board at the front under the window, the finishing table behind it.

**The makers:** **Ciro Lanzetta**, *maestro*, who cuts. The jackets are made by
three *giacchettaie* — women who make coats at their own kitchen tables across
the Chiaia and Vomero, the way Naples has always organised this work — and by
one *pantalonaio* for trousers. Ciro cuts and fits; he has not sewn a whole
coat himself since 1994 and says so.

**Proposition:** a coat built out of as little as it can be built out of.

## The register argument (recorded because it was the call)

- **Native hour.** The board under one lamp, after the shutters are down. The
  interior of a coat has no daylight condition — it is only ever seen opened,
  lit, and pointed at.
- **Not chiaroscuro for its own sake.** Every plate is *generously* lit; the
  darkness is the room around the board, never a veil over the subject. The
  cloth, the canvas, and the hands are fully modelled. A murky plate here would
  hide exactly the thing the site exists to show.
- **Distance.** The nearest Nocturne siblings are Italian but nothing like
  this: Sant'Agata is a Cremonese violin bench (wood, varnish, blue), Alla Luna
  a Murano furnace (glass, heat, orange). Cloth, canvas, and thread are a
  different material world, and the argumentative Neapolitan voice — a house
  that defines itself *against* another city's tailoring — has no sibling.

## Binding palette — materials from the world

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Notte | `#14120e` | Page ground — the room with the shutters down |
| Panno | `#211c15` | The cloth on the board — panels, raised fields |
| Crine | `#4a4133` | Horsehair canvas — rules, hairlines, labels |
| Gesso | `#eae4d6` | Tailor's chalk — body text; **the light of this site** |
| Corallo | `#c05a41` | Torre del Greco coral — the ONE applied colour |
| Corallo-deep | `#9c452f` | Coral, pressed / hover |

**The corallo rule** (this register's version of the metal accent): Nocturne
asks for one material that behaves like light. Here that is **chalk**, not
metal — gesso is what the tailor draws with and what the page reads in. The
single *applied* colour is coral, and it appears only where a marking thread
would: the active layer in the signature, the basting line, focus rings, one
primary action per view. If coral exceeds ~5% of a frame it has become
decoration; pull back.

Body text is gesso on notte (verified ≈15:1).

## Signature geometry

**The crescent.** The *barchetta* pocket's rising curve is the only curve on
the site — section thresholds, the wordmark tick, the favicon. Everything else
is drawn square, because everything else in tailoring is a straight line
between two chalk marks.

start yours.*

## The signature interaction — "L'interno": the coat opened

The one bold spend, built and critiqued first, fused with the order form per
doctrine. A large SVG of a jacket **held open the way a tailor shows you one**:
the right front seen from outside (lapel, barchetta pocket, buttons), the left
front swung open to show its **inside**. The strata a buyer never sees are
drawn as nested, hatched regions — cloth, floating canvas, haircloth chest
piece, lining — and they add and subtract live.

Four controls, all real decisions a client actually makes:

1. **Costruzione** (Sfoderata / Mezza fodera / Foderata) → how many strata
   exist at all. Sfoderata shows bare seam allowances and taped edges; foderata
   covers the interior in a lining field and the canvas retreats behind it.
2. **Tela** (Tela intera / Mezza tela) → how far the floating canvas runs down
   the front; the hatched region grows or stops at the chest.
3. **Spalla** (Spalla a camicia / Mappina / Con rollino) → redraws the shoulder
   line and the sleeve head, from gathered ripples to a slight roll.
4. **Impuntura** (Nessuna / 6 mm / 12 mm) → the hand pick-stitch appears along
   the lapel and front edge at the chosen distance, drawn stitch by stitch in
   coral.

All four feed one model, so the section, the shoulder, and the stitching
**move together in one redraw**. A live readout states the coat's finished
weight in grams, the hours of hand-work, the number of fittings, and the price
— updating with the drawing. **"Comincia questa giacca"** carries the whole
spec into the order form and scrolls to it.

**JS off / `?nofx` / reduced motion:** the SVG renders at a sensible default
(Sfoderata, Tela intera, Spalla a camicia, 6 mm) fully drawn with the readout
printed, and all four controls present as real radios — the form still submits
the chosen spec. The reveal animation is gated behind an `html.js` class set by
an inline head script, so a JS-off visitor gets the finished composition rather
than content stranded at opacity 0. *(This is a floor a sibling build failed on
first measurement; it is now checked deliberately.)*

## How the site takes an order — the order form is the primary path

A jacket here runs **€2,930–€4,700** — under the $5,000 line, so commitment 7
makes the order form the primary path, not a "write to us" gesture.

**Ordinare** collects a real, structured commission:

- **La giacca:** construction, canvas, shoulder, pick-stitch — pre-filled from
  the signature — plus buttons (horn / corozo / mother-of-pearl), vents, and
  the cloth (a real merchant bunch: a 280 g tropical wool, a 340 g flannel, a
  310 g hopsack, or "bring your own length").
- **Le misure:** height, weight, chest, waist, shoulder, sleeve, and the
  jacket length they wear now — with the honest note that Ciro will not cut
  from posted measurements alone and that the first *prova* has to happen in
  person, in Naples or on a trunk visit.
- **Quando:** the date needed, and which trunk city (Naples / London / New York
  / Tokyo) if not Naples.
- **Contact:** name, email, city.

It hands off by **mailto** (`sartoria@barchetta.it`) with `enctype="text/plain"`
so every field lands legibly as an order request — never a card, never a
checkout.

**Pronte** carries a few finished coats available now, each with its real spec
and a "Chiedere" mailto — shown editorially, never a shop grid.

## Copy voice

Argumentative, specific, slightly amused — a house that defines itself against
Savile Row and enjoys it. Grams, millimetres, hours, dates. "A canvas is there
to hold a shape the wearer's own chest could hold by itself." "Three hundred
and forty grams, and no shoulder padding at all — the shoulder is the sleeve
head and nothing else." Never "sprezzatura", never "effortless elegance",
never Italian-lifestyle filler, never a nostalgic-Naples postcard.

## Imagery — the Barchetta recipe

**One recipe: a single warm work lamp fully and generously modelling the
subject against a deep near-black room.** Nocturne band, target mean luma
20–45 — rich, never murky. The site exists to show interiors of coats; an
underexposed plate defeats it. Coral appears only in real thread. People are
the maker and the makers' hands — never a model in a suit, never street-style.

Constant batch phrase: *"lit by a single warm work lamp that fully and
generously models the subject, soft amber fill, deep near-black surroundings,
worn wooden tailor's board, rich and legible rather than murky, documentary
craft photography, shallow depth of field, no text, no logos."*

Shot list: `hero-board`, `crine` (macro, pad-stitched chest piece),
`imbastitura` (human presence — hands basting), `spalla` (the gathered sleeve
head), `barchetta` (the pocket, macro), `maestro` (Ciro at the board, face lit
by the lamp), `prova` (a basted coat on the form), `pronte` (three finished
coats), `ordinare` (the order table from above).

## Floors (binding)

Complete with JS off (the order form submits, the signature renders drawn);
visible corallo focus states; body contrast ≥ 7:1 measured, not eyeballed;
full keyboard paths (signature controls are real radios); reduced-motion gets
the finished static drawing; every image ≤500KB; dedicated mobile pass.

## Never

A model in a finished suit · street-style or pitti-peacock imagery ·
"sprezzatura" · nostalgic-Naples postcards (Vesuvius, washing lines, scooters)
· murky underexposed plates (the interior must read) · coral as a broad fill ·
rounded floating cards · a shop grid · a generic checkout · a "write to us"
gesture in place of the real order form.

## Build status (completed 2026-07-31, full critique loop)

All 9 planned finals generated plus 2 extra finished coats (11 total); no CSS
placeholders needed. Optimized to `public/images/` as full (≤1536px) plus
`-800` variants, every file ≤500KB (largest 480KB).

Measured mean luma, all inside or above the Nocturne 20–45 band and none
underexposed: ordinare 64, hero-board 60, crine 60, imbastitura 51, maestro 46,
barchetta 43, pronte-c 36, pronte-b 30, pronte 27, spalla 25, prova 22. The
optimizer's luma flag was retuned for this register (it now flags < 20 as too
dark, rather than inheriting Nipis's bright-Aubade threshold of 90, which would
have condemned every correct plate here). Human presence: hands basting, and
Ciro at the board lit by the lamp.

Signature "L'interno" built and critiqued first (3 passes):

1. The opened front was rotated the wrong way — `rotate(-8)` swung it *into*
   the right front instead of outward, so the two panels overlapped into one
   unreadable shape. Corrected to a clockwise swing about the top of the front
   edge, which reads as a coat held open.
2. Both panels were far too narrow (tall slabs, not jacket fronts) and the
   whole plate was too dark to read against the ground. Widened, re-proportioned,
   and every cloth/stroke value lifted.
3. The lapel rendered as a zigzag; rebuilt as a proper notch lapel with a
   dashed roll line and a gorge seam, and the right front given a real armhole
   curve for the sleeve-head ripples to sit on.

Verified interactively (`tools/shot-el.mjs`) that the three constructions
genuinely differ: **sfoderata** shows canvas, haircloth chest piece, and coral
bound seams; **mezza fodera** closes an indigo lining over the top half;
**foderata** covers the whole interior and the canvas ghosts behind it. That is
the site's claim, so it was checked rather than assumed.

The model also produced an honest and useful fact the copy now leads on: the
**cheapest** coat is the fully lined one (€2,930, 865 g) and the **dearest** is
the unlined one (€4,700, 740 g), because removing the lining adds fourteen
hours of hand-finishing. Subtraction is the expensive direction.

Floors verified:
- **JS-off** — page complete, coat fully drawn with readout printed, all four
  control groups showing their `:checked` state without script. The reveal is
  gated behind `html.js` from the start on this build (the failure mode was
  found on Nipis and carried across deliberately).
- **Contrast** — computed, not eyeballed. Five values failed first measurement:
  the whisper label, the nav order link, button ink on coral, and the plate
  labels. `--crine-lo` was opened from `#6b5f4b` to `#918468` and `--corallo`
  from `#c05a41` to `#cc6a4e` until all cleared 4.5:1 on *both* grounds. Body
  gesso-on-notte 14.8:1, lede 9.3/8.6:1, captions 5.9/5.6:1.
- Keyboard paths through real radios; coral focus rings; reduced-motion gets
  the finished static drawing; all images ≤500KB; mobile pass at 390px.

Also corrected: two rail coats were described as finished indigo and pale grey
but illustrated with a basted coat and a brown shoulder. Rather than rewriting
the copy to match the stock imagery, two matching coats were generated and the
basted-coat plate moved to the fitting chapter where it belongs.

`npm run build` passes clean.
