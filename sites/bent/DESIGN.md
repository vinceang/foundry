# BENT — Design source of truth

*Written 2026-08-21, before any component. A wooden roller coaster works in
Northumberland County, Pennsylvania: first light on raw timber, a structure that
is built to move, and a craft that is never finished.*

## The one-line brief

**A wooden coaster is not a structure that holds still. It is a structure that
is allowed to move, and someone has to keep deciding how much.**

## Identity

**Name:** **BENT** — the transverse A-frame section that is the fundamental unit
of a wooden coaster's structure. Timber framers have called it a bent for
centuries; coaster crews still do. The word also describes what happens to the
track itself: nine layers of southern yellow pine, laminated and bent by hand
around every curve. One ordinary trade word doing the whole job of the brand,
and it teaches the visitor the vocabulary in the first scroll.

**The light: early morning, before the park opens.** Chosen from the subject,
not from a category. This trade's real hour is the walk-through at first light —
the circuit inspected on foot before a single guest arrives, sun coming in low
and level through the lattice, the structure throwing a hundred feet of shadow
and completely empty. It is the only time the ride can be seen as a *building*
rather than as a ride. A night register would be a lie: nobody works a wooden
coaster in the dark, and the chiaroscuro plate would be borrowed from Shokunin's
lamp rather than argued from this world.

**Not the amusement park.** The trap here is enormous and it must be named
before anything is drawn: roller coasters connote fun, families, screaming,
Orlando. **This site is about the structure, and the structure is a serious
piece of timber engineering.** No riders' faces. No motion blur of a passing
train. No stripes, no pennants, no midway. If a screen could carry a park's logo
without looking wrong, delete it.

**Distance from the collection.** Checked against all twenty-four built sites:

- **Not Traccia.** Traccia's thesis is *the road writes the frame* — terrain
  specifying a product. The obvious coaster signature was "the hillside writes
  the ride," and it is therefore closed. BENT argues movement, not geometry.
- **Not Velum.** Velum's page *is* a flight profile down a rail. A ride profile
  on a rail is closed for the same reason.
- **Not Tyri.** Tyri assembles a log corner as you scroll. A structure that
  assembles on scroll is closed. BENT's structure is already built; what changes
  is how much it moves.
- **Not Malmfuru.** Malmfuru owns tree rings and timber provenance.
- **Not the four "annotated artifact" sites** (Suntoku, Shokunin, Casa Vicente,
  Schwarzwald). No exploded diagram with callouts. That device is spent.

**Place:** A yard on the **West Branch of the Susquehanna, Northumberland
County, Pennsylvania** — hardwood ridges, river flats, and the part of America
where the wooden coaster actually survived. The trade has real roots in this
county and has never entirely left it.

**Founded:** 1946. A framing carpenter came home from the war to a shop that
built barns and covered bridges, and took the first coaster contract because
nobody else in the county would touch the drawings. Third generation.

**The crew:** eleven people. Not a factory. Four carpenters, two welders for the
hardware, a draughtsman, a millwright, and three who do nothing but walk and
replace track.

**Proposition:** we build them, and then we never stop building them.

## The homage — and why it is the site's spine

Wooden coasters are disappearing, and the way they disappear is the interesting
part. They are rarely demolished. They are **retracked**: the wooden running
surface is replaced with steel rail on the original wooden structure. The
skeleton survives and the craft does not.

At the same time, a wooden coaster that is *kept* wooden is a Ship of Theseus.
Track laminate wears out and is replaced continuously, board by board, for
decades. A ride from 1947 may contain almost none of its 1947 wood.

So the site holds one honest, uncomfortable idea:

> **The ride is not the wood. The ride is the replacing.**

That is the homage. Not nostalgia for old timber — respect for the crew that
turns up every spring and rebuilds it again.

## Money, and therefore the order form

Two paths, per commitment 8, and the split is real rather than decorative:

- **A ride is a commission.** Multi-million, two years, drawings, a site survey,
  a state inspector. This is genuinely above the $5,000 line, so it is
  **commission by correspondence** — a real letter, not a form.
- **Salvage is an order form.** When a section is retracked or a ride comes
  down, the yard keeps the track. A **section of laminated running track, nine
  layers of southern yellow pine, with its provenance** — which ride, which
  curve, which years it was under load, and roughly how many trains crossed it —
  sells for **$280–$3,400** depending on length and whether the steel running
  rail is still on it.

That is under the line, so salvage gets a **proper structured order form** as
the site's primary conversion. It also carries the homage: the thing you can
actually buy is the thing that was replaced.

The form takes an order request by mailto. It never touches a card.

## Binding palette — materials from the world

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| **Silver** | `#b9b3a7` | Weathered pine, forty years in the weather — the page ground |
| **Ash** | `#cfcabf` | Lighter weathered face, panels and quiet fields |
| **Sky** | `#8fa3ad` | First-light sky through the lattice, cool counterpoint |
| **Creosote** | `#3a332c` | Treated timber and shadow — body ink, rules |
| **Iron** | `#1b1815` | Bolt, plate, structural dark |
| **Fresh cut** | `#c98a3c` | **Southern yellow pine, newly milled — the ONE accent** |

**The fresh-cut rule.** This is the palette decision the whole site turns on. A
wooden coaster is silver-grey almost everywhere, because the timber has been
weathering for decades. The only gold in the frame is **wood that was replaced
recently** — a new track laminate, a new ledger board, a fresh sister on a bent.

So the accent is not a brand colour. **It marks where the craft has recently
touched the ride.** It appears only there: the active state, focus rings, the
one primary action per view, the newest boards in the signature, the marks in
the salvage record. Over roughly 5% of a frame it stops reading as new work and
starts reading as decoration; pull back.

Note the deliberate distance from siblings: Tyri's `#a8701f` is a golden brown
resin and Traccia's `#a8452a` is a red oxide. **Fresh cut is a lighter, rawer,
sawn-timber gold**, and its ground is a *cool* weathered silver rather than
Tyri's warm pine tar or Traccia's winter haze.

Body text is creosote on silver. Verify ≥ 7:1 by measurement, not by eye.

## Type

- **Fraunces** — the display voice. A soft serif with genuine American warmth
  and a slightly worked, hand-cut quality that suits sawn timber and refuses the
  fashion-magazine trap. Regular weight only, tracked slightly wide.
- **IBM Plex Sans** — the working voice. Squared, engineering-drawn, unfussy.
- **IBM Plex Mono** — measurements only: deflection in inches, board counts,
  years under load, train counts. Numbers that matter are set here and written
  out in full.

None of the three appears on a sibling. This is also the collection's **first
all-English site** — no foreign vocabulary to lean on, which means the copy has
to carry the world by itself.

## Signature geometry

**The bent.** The A-frame silhouette in elevation — two raking legs, a
horizontal ledger, a cross-brace. It is the only ornament: chapter marks, the
favicon, the checked state, list marks. Everything else is drawn as a line,
because a coaster structure is nothing but lines. Zero border-radius.

## The signature interaction — "The Sway"

The one bold spend. Built and critiqued first.

**The claim, which is true and which almost nobody knows:** a wooden coaster's
structure is *designed to move*. Under a loaded train, a tall bent deflects
several inches laterally and returns. This is not wear and it is not a fault —
it is the design. A wooden coaster built rigid would destroy itself, because
there would be nowhere for the energy to go. The flex is the engineering.

**The interaction.** A large scale front elevation of one bent, drawn as line
work. The visitor drags a train across it — or scrubs a slider — and the bent
**leans, and comes back**. Live readouts in Plex Mono beside it:

- **Deflection** — inches at the top chord, live.
- **Load** — the train's weight over that bent at this instant.
- **Return** — how long the bent takes to come back to plumb.

Two controls, and they are the argument:

1. **Train weight** — empty / half / full. A full train moves the structure
   noticeably more, and the number says so.
2. **Timber age** — new build / twenty years / fifty years. Older timber has
   different stiffness, and the crew compensates by sistering and re-bolting.

**The conflict that cannot be designed away** (Traccia's DESIGN.md has the right
instinct here — the signature must contain a real tension): **there is a band.**
Too little movement and the joints take the shock and split. Too much and the
ride is loose, the train fights the track, and it will valley. The readout shows
the band, and the visitor can drive the bent out of it in both directions. Going
*too stiff* is the surprising failure and it is the point of the whole thing.

Reduced motion gets the composition at full deflection, static, with the band
and figures rendered. JS off gets the same as a drawn SVG. It is never a blank
box.

## How the site takes an order

**Salvage — the primary path, a real form.** Fields: which piece (a picked
section from the current inventory), length, whether the running rail stays on,
delivery or collection, and the buyer's details. Each inventory item carries its
record — ride, curve, years under load, approximate train count — because
commitment 10 says a specimen carries what the small form had no room for.

**Commission — a letter.** Name, park or operator, the site, the drawings if
they exist. No fields pretending a ride can be specified in a textarea.

## Imagery — the BENT recipe

Constant art-direction phrase across the whole batch:

> *"early morning light just after sunrise raking low through a large wooden
> roller coaster structure, weathered silver-grey southern yellow pine timber,
> documentary architectural photography, deep clear shadows, cool blue shadow
> and warm low sun, no people unless specified, no text, no logos, no signage,
> no lettering"*

Six plates, no more:

1. **Hero** — the structure seen along its length at first light, empty, the
   lattice throwing long shadows. Landscape 3:2.
2. **The bent** — one A-frame section shot square on from below, legs raking out
   of frame. Portrait 2:3.
3. **The laminate** — extreme close-up of the cut end of track: nine distinct
   layers of pine, the steel running rail on top. Landscape 3:2.
4. **The walk** — a carpenter walking the catwalk in the low sun, seen from
   behind or in profile, tools on the belt. **The human plate.** Portrait 2:3.
5. **Fresh timber** — newly milled southern yellow pine boards stacked against
   the weathered structure, the gold against the silver. Landscape 3:2.
6. **Salvage** — a cut section of laminated track lying on a workbench, its
   layers visible, in daylight. Landscape 3:2.

Exposure: this is a daylight register. **Mean luma floor 65.** Measure it.
A murky coaster plate is a defect, not a mood.

## Never

Riders' faces · a train in motion blur · stripes, pennants, bunting, a midway ·
"thrill", "adrenaline", "the ride of your life" · any park's marks · a second
accent · rounded corners · uniform card grids · a tracked label above every
heading · nostalgia as the argument — the crew is working now, not remembering.

## Copy voice

Declarative, short, unhedged. A carpenter explaining something to someone who
asked a good question. Measurements in inches and feet, because that is what
this trade uses. The one flaw admitted formally, once: **wood is more work than
steel, and it always will be.** Say it plainly and let it stand.
