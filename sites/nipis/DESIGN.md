# NIPIS — Design source of truth

*Written 2026-07-31, before any component. A bespoke barong atelier in the
Aubade register: piña and jusi worked to commission in Lumban, Laguna.*

## The one-line brief

**The only formal garment in the world made to be seen through.**

Every other formal tradition hides its construction — the Neapolitan coat
buries a canvas, the dinner jacket lines itself in silk. A barong Tagalog is
the opposite argument: it is worn untucked, unlined, over a plain undershirt,
in a cloth so sheer the body reads through it. There is nowhere to hide a bad
seam. That is the whole site.

## Identity

**Name:** **Nipis** — from Tagalog *manipis*, thin. *Tela de nipis* is the
historical name for the family of sheer Philippine cloths — piña, jusi,
piña-seda — woven fine enough to hold to a window and read light through.
The name is the thesis: thinness is not a weakness of the cloth, it is the
achievement.

**Series:** **Aubade** — the morning voice, bent (as Veta and Ibushi bent it)
from hospitality toward a *maker*. The register is not a choice of mood here;
it is forced by the subject. A cloth whose entire quality is judged by holding
it up to daylight cannot be photographed under a Nocturne lamp — backlight *is*
the craft. Tropical morning through a capiz-shell window, before the Laguna
heat arrives.

**Place:** **Lumban, Laguna**, on the southern shore of Laguna de Bay — the
Philippine embroidery town, where barong have been hand-embroidered for
generations. The cloth itself arrives from **Kalibo, Aklan**, the last place
where *piña* is still hand-scraped from red Bisaya pineapple leaves and knotted
fibre to fibre before it ever reaches a loom. The site follows that journey:
a leaf on one island becomes a panel across one man's chest on another.

**The makers:** two hands, two trades, one bench. **Estrella Bituin**, the
*mamburda* (embroiderer), who works the *pechera* — thirty years of calado.
**Rodel Bituin**, the *mananahi* (tailor), who cuts and joins. A barong takes
them between six weeks and five months depending on the panel.

**Proposition:** a barong cut to one man and embroidered by one pair of hands,
in cloth you can hold to a window.

## The register argument (recorded because it was the call)

- **Native hour.** Piña is *graded by light*. Both the weaver and the buyer
  judge it by lifting it against a window. The subject's native condition is
  bright, soft, backlit tropical daylight — the Aubade band, high luma. A dark
  register would not merely be a different mood, it would make the product
  illegible.
- **Not a hotel.** Aubade's hospitality rituals are replaced by the acts of the
  craft, per the Veta precedent: hands scraping fibre, a needle entering sheer
  cloth, a finished barong held to a window. No lifestyle, no wedding models.
- **Distance.** The Foundry has no Philippine site, no garment site, and no
  textile site of any kind. The nearest neighbours by register (Veta's Oaxacan
  bay, Ibushi's Hida houses) are wood-and-foam workshops on other continents.

**The dahon rule** (this site's version of the metal accent): the leaf green is
the single applied colour and appears only where the plant itself would —
the active motif in the signature, link underlines, focus rings, one primary
button per view. Everything else is fibre, cloth, and ink. If green exceeds
~6% of a frame it has become decoration; pull back. This is deliberately held
*dark* — the Aubade profile lists "sage green on every surface" as an instant
fail, and the defence is that this green is a leaf at depth, never a wash.

Body text is tinta on liniwan (verified ≈14:1).

## Type

- **Petrona** (variable) — the display voice. A warm Latin-rooted old-style
  serif with real hairline contrast in the thins, which is the point: the
  letterforms thin out the way the cloth does. Carries statements, chapter
  openings, the barong numbers.
- **Karla** — the working voice: measurements in centimetres, cloth grades,
  stitch counts, the order form, tracked-caps whisper labels (11px, 600,
  .3em, in kayumanggi).

Neither face appears on any sibling site (Veta has Fraunces/Space Grotesk,
Aresta Archivo/Newsreader, Velum Spectral/Plex Mono, Tyri Schibsted).

## Signature geometry

**The openwork lattice.** *Calado* is made by drawing threads out of the
ground and whipping what remains into a lattice — the ornament is the absence.
So the site's only ornament is a thin drawn-thread rule: a hairline with
regular gaps, used at section thresholds and in the wordmark. Nothing else is
decorated. Everything is drawn square except the embroidery itself.

## The signature interaction — "Ang pechera": the panel drawn on sheer cloth

The one bold spend, built and critiqued first, and fused with the order form
per doctrine. A large SVG of the barong's **pechera** — the embroidered front
panel — sits on the daylight ground. **Critically, it is drawn on a
translucent ground**: behind the cloth layer sits a faint warm under-layer
(the wearer's *camisa de chino* and shoulder line). The cloth's opacity is a
live variable, so when the buyer changes cloth, *the sheerness visibly
changes* — you watch the body read through more or less. Nothing else in the
collection does this, and no other subject could.

Four controls change the panel **in one motion**:

1. **Tela** (Jusi / Piña-seda / Piña liniwan) → sets the ground's opacity and
   warmth. Liniwan is the sheerest and the costliest; the under-layer surfaces
   through it. It also sets how strongly the **sombrado** shadow reads, since
   shadow work is stitched on the reverse and shows *through* the cloth.
2. **Motif** (Sampaguita vine / Anahaw fan / Espiga / pure Calado lattice) →
   redraws the embroidery paths across the panel.
3. **Okasyon** (Araw-araw / Kasal / Panauhin — everyday / wedding / state) →
   sets the panel's **spread**: how wide across the chest and how far down
   toward the hem the burda runs.
4. **Calado** (a density range) → how much of the ground is cut away into
   openwork lattice; at high density the under-layer reads strongly through
   the cut ground.

All four feed one SVG whose paths interpolate between named control-point
sets, so ground, motif, spread, and openwork **move together in one redraw** —
not four widgets. A live readout states cloth, motif, panel drop in
centimetres, estimated stitch hours, and the price band, updating with the
drawing. **"Simulan ang barong na ito"** (begin this barong) carries the whole
spec straight into the order form and scrolls to it.

**JS off / `?nofx` / reduced motion:** the SVG renders at a sensible default
(Piña-seda, Sampaguita, Kasal, medium calado) fully drawn with the readout
printed, and all four controls present as real radios and a range — the form
still submits the chosen spec. No animation, never a broken frame.

## How the site takes an order — the order form is the primary path

A hand-embroidered barong here runs **₱9,800–₱119,100 (roughly $170–$2,035)**
— entirely under $5,000, so commitment 7 makes the order form the site's
*primary* path, not a "write to us" gesture.

**Magpatahi** collects a real, structured commission:

- **Ang barong:** cloth, motif, occasion, calado density — pre-filled from the
  signature — plus collar (regular / mandarin), cuff (barrel / French), and
  hem length.
- **Ang sukat (the measurements):** chest, shoulder, sleeve, nape-to-hem,
  neck — in centimetres, with a plain-language note on how to take each, and
  an alternative "send me a shirt that fits" option, which is what the atelier
  actually prefers.
- **Kailan (when):** the date it is needed. This matters because the panel
  determines the wait — a state-occasion pechera is five months of needle.
- **Contact:** name, email, city.

It hands off by **mailto** (`palihan@nipis.ph`) with `enctype="text/plain"` so
every field lands legibly as an order request — never a card, never a
checkout. It obeys Nipis's type, palette, and voice throughout.

**Ang aparador** carries a few finished barong ready now, each with its real
spec (cloth, motif, sukat, hours at the needle) and a "Tanungin" mailto —
shown editorially, never a shop grid.

## Copy voice

Declarative, specific, numeric. Fibre grades, stitch hours, centimetres,
months of waiting. "Forty metres of piña liniwan a month, on a good month."
"A wedding pechera is roughly 340 hours; we take six a year." Never
"timeless elegance," never "heritage craftsmanship," never a colonial-nostalgia
register. Tagalog terms are used because they are the correct terms, always
glossed once, never as flavour.

## Imagery — the Nipis recipe

**One recipe: bright, soft, backlit tropical morning daylight; sheer cloth
read against the light; the fibre and the needle fully modelled.** High luma
(target mean ~110–165) — this is the brightest site in the collection by
argument, because backlight is the craft. Green appears only as actual plant
matter. People are the two makers at work — hands scraping fibre, a needle
entering cloth, a face lit by the window — never wedding models, never a
posed groom.

Constant batch phrase: *"bright soft backlit tropical morning daylight through
a wooden capiz-shell window, sheer ivory pineapple-fibre cloth held against
the light, pale ivory and ecru and raw fibre tones with deep green only as
real leaf, documentary craft photography, shallow depth of field, no text, no
logos."*

Shot list:
1. `hero-window` — a finished barong on a wooden hanger held against a bright
   capiz window; the light comes through the cloth and the pechera reads as
   shadow.
2. `kiskis` — human presence: hands drawing a shard across a pineapple leaf,
   fibre separating, green leaf and pale fibre together.
3. `hibla` — material close-up: knotted piña fibres, hair-fine, on dark wood.
4. `habi` — the loom: piña warp under window light, the shed open.
5. `burda` — human presence: the embroiderer's hands and needle working calado
   on sheer cloth stretched in a frame, face just lit at the edge of frame.
6. `mananahi` — the tailor at the bench with shears and a cut panel.
7. `aparador` — two or three finished barong on wooden hangers in daylight.
8. `sukat` — the order chapter: tape measure, chalk, a spec sheet, a folded
   length of piña on a worktable.

Placeholders (if any) use solid jusi / bastos / dahon blocks at the intended
crop — never grey boxes.

## Build status (completed 2026-07-31, full critique loop)

All 8 planned finals generated plus 2 extra rack variants (10 total); no CSS
placeholders were needed. Optimized to `public/images/` as full (≤1536px) plus
`-800` variants, JPEG q72–78, every file ≤500KB (largest 448KB) — `optimize.sh`
steps quality down until the cap is met rather than leaving it to chance.

Measured mean luma, all inside the bright Aubade band (none murky): hero-window
160, aparador 166, kiskis 144, burda 141, sukat 139, habi 138, mananahi 130,
aparador-b 127, aparador-c 119, hibla 109. The two human-presence shots (hands
at the kiskis; Estrella at the embroidery frame) plus the maker portrait anchor
the fibre, burda, and mananahi chapters, per doctrine.

Signature "Ang pechera" built and critiqued first (4 passes). What changed
across them, recorded because the reasoning is reusable:

1. The garment silhouette read as a lumpy coat-hanger; replaced with a proper
   flat-laid torso and softened shoulder caps.
2. `calado` first rendered as a grid of dots — a checkerboard, not needlework.
   Rebuilt from what calado physically *is*: threads drawn OUT of the ground in
   both directions, with a wider opening where two removals cross. The removed
   threads are cut from the cloth via an SVG mask, so the under-layer really
   does read through them.
3. `sombrado` was invisible, because it sits *under* the cloth (it is stitched
   on the reverse) and the cloth's own opacity was extinguishing it. Fixed by
   making it a filled, offset, blurred stratum and raising its opacity per
   cloth grade.
4. The panel over-ran the shoulder line at the widest setting; `panauhin`
   half-width pulled to 78 and the burda top moved down to y=94.

Verified interactively (`tools/shot-el.mjs`, added this build) that changing
cloth genuinely changes sheerness: jusi renders near-opaque, piña liniwan lets
the camisa de chino and the body read through and opens the lattice. That is
the site's whole claim, so it was checked rather than assumed.

The order form is the primary path (₱9,800–₱119,100, well under $5,000): four
fieldsets (barong / sukat / kailan / kayo) in the craft's own nouns, mailto
handoff (`palihan@nipis.ph`, `text/plain`), no card, works JS-off. "Simulan ang
barong na ito" carries the drawn spec into it and focuses the form.

Floors verified:
- **JS-off** — caught and fixed a real failure here: `.rise` was `opacity: 0`
  by default and only revealed by script, so a JS-off visitor got a blank page.
  The reveal is now gated behind `html.js`, set by an inline head script, so
  JS-off gets the finished composition. The signature renders fully drawn with
  its readout printed and all four controls live (`:checked` styling needs no
  script).
- **Contrast** — computed, not eyeballed, and three values failed their stated
  floors on first measurement. Alphas were solved against the *jusi* ground
  (the darker of the two page grounds): body 15.2:1, lede 7.8/7.1:1, captions
  5.2/4.9:1, whisper labels 6.5/5.6:1, buttons 8.3:1, footer fine 6.7:1.
- Keyboard paths through real radios and a range; dahon focus rings;
  reduced-motion gets the finished static drawing; all images ≤500KB; mobile
  pass at 390px (panel stays large, controls go two-up, readout stacks).

`npm run build` passes clean.
