# FÀIRE — Design source of truth

*Written 2026-08-07, before any component. The keeper's house at Rubha na
Fàire, Wester Ross, in a newly proposed register: **Vigil**.*

## The one-line brief

**You sleep in the house of a light that no longer needs anyone — and it still
crosses the ceiling every twenty seconds.**

## Identity

**Name:** **Fàire** — Scottish Gaelic for *watching, keeping watch*; also the
horizon, and the coming of dawn. One word for the whole subject: the watch
that was kept here for a hundred and thirty-one years, the horizon it was kept
against, and the dawn that ended each one. The headland is **Rubha na Fàire**,
"the point of the watch"; the house takes its name from the point.

**Series:** **Vigil — a fifth register, proposed by this build.** The backlog
guessed "a Vesper," written before Vesper existed. Now that it does, it is
demonstrably the wrong answer, and the reason is a single clean sentence:

> **Vesper's subjects are built on not being seen. A lighthouse exists in
> order to be seen.** Same darkness, opposite purpose.

Nocturne is closer and still wrong. Nocturne is *a lamp in a room* — one
artifact held in reverence, emerging from a void, the darkness a material like
lacquer. This is *a lamp in a world*: the light is outside, often miles off, it
falls on weather rather than on an object, the composition is vast where
Nocturne's is scarce, and the feeling is **vigilance** rather than reverence.
Nothing here is a museum artifact on a void, and building it as one would be
the register failure the profile now warns against.

So: a fifth quality of light, and the naming family makes the case by itself.
Vespers and **Vigils** are the two night offices — evening and the deep of
night. The full profile is at
[`docs/vigil-taste-profile.md`](../../docs/vigil-taste-profile.md).

**Place:** **Rubha na Fàire**, a low granite headland on the Wester Ross coast
north of Loch Ewe, looking out past the Minch. The light is a shore station:
tower, two keepers' houses, a walled garden that has never grown anything, and
a stone jetty in the only landing on that side of the point. Nearest village
eleven miles; the last four are a single track the estate does not grit.

**Established:** 1857, by the Stevensons. **Automated:** 1988. Manned for
**131 years**, and the site says so in those words because the number is the
whole story.

**The light:** `Fl(2) W 20s · 39 m · 24 M` — two white flashes every twenty
seconds, thirty-nine metres above high water, seen twenty-four nautical miles
off. That entry is real in its format and is the site's spine.

**The house:** the principal keeper's house, sleeping four. Two bedrooms, a
kitchen with the original range, a sitting room facing the light. Let by the
week.

**Proposition:** a week inside a working instrument.

## The people — and there are faces, deliberately

A world nobody inhabits reads as a rendering, and this one was inhabited
around the clock for 131 years. Three faces, each with a job and a number:

- **Iain MacAulay**, the last principal keeper. Came in 1971, switched the
  watch off in 1988, still lives eleven miles away. He is the one who can say
  what the room sounded like.
- **Mòrag NicLeòid**, the attendant. Services the light four times a year —
  lamp changer, charge controller, the panes. She is the reason it still runs
  and she has never met most of the people who see it.
- **Dòmhnall Grant**, who brings guests and everything else in by boat when
  the track is out, which is about nine weeks a year.

## Money, and therefore the form

The house lets at **£1,150–£1,690 a week** by season. Well under the $5,000
line, so commitment 7 makes a **real structured booking form the primary
path** — not a "write to us" gesture. It takes the week, the party, and the
honest questions (can you drive the track, do you need the boat), and hands
off an enquiry by mailto. It never takes a card or a deposit.

## Binding palette — materials from the world

| Token | Value | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| **Muir** | `#0a1014` | The sea at night — the page ground |
| **Sgòth** | `#141d24` | Cloud on the water — panels, raised fields |
| **Clach** | `#3a4650` | Wet granite — rules, hairlines, borders |
| **Ceò** | `#8fa0aa` | Sea haze — secondary text, captions |
| **Aol** | `#e8e6df` | Lime harling — body text; the tower itself |
| **Solas** | `#f2c46a` | **The light.** The ONE warm note |

**The solas rule:** *solas* is not an accent chosen for taste — it is the
light, which is the entire function of the place. It appears only where the
light truly falls: the beam, the lit window, the active state, the focus ring,
one primary action per view, and the flash in the signature. Over ~5% of a
frame it has stopped being a light and become decoration.

Body text is aol on muir. Verify ≥ 7:1 by measurement, and solve secondary
values against **sgòth**, the darker second ground, not against muir.

## Type

- **Young Serif** — the display voice. A weighted old-style with a carved,
  slightly weathered face; it has the heft of a granite tower where a
  high-contrast didone would have the wrong thinness. Statements and chapter
  openings only (it has one weight, and that is the correct amount).
- **Barlow** — the working voice. A quiet, faintly squared grotesque with
  numerals that hold a column, which this site needs constantly: positions,
  characters, elevations, ranges, seconds, metres, nautical miles.

Neither appears on a sibling (Tyri: Besley/Schibsted; Traccia: Bodoni
Moda/Titillium; the rest all different again).

## Signature geometry

**The sector.** A narrow wedge from a point — the shape a light actually
throws, and a real navigational object: lights carry coloured sectors marking
safe water from foul. It is the site's only ornament: the mark, the favicon,
the hotspot pins, the chapter rule's terminal, list marks. Everything else is
drawn as a line or a circle, because a chart is drawn in lines and circles.
Zero border-radius.

## The opening move — the List of Lights entry

**Not** a tracked caps label. Every light in the world is published in the
Admiralty List of Lights in one fixed format, and each chapter opens with
**this light's entry, with the field that chapter is about set in the light
itself**:

`A 4126 · Rubha na Fàire · 57°52′.4 N 5°48′.7 W · Fl(2) W 20s · 39 m · 24 M`

| Chapter | The field lit |
|---|---|
| An Solas | `Fl(2) W 20s` — the character |
| An Tùr | `39 m` — the elevation, and the 96 steps to it |
| An Taigh | `A 4126` — the station number, because the house is part of the station |
| Na Daoine | `1857 – 1988` — the years it was manned |
| Fuireach | `57°52′.4 N 5°48′.7 W` — the position you are driving to |

It earns its place because it is the subject's own published record, it is
identical every time except for what is lit, and the lit field is the one the
chapter uses. Recorded here so it holds site-wide.

Gaelic, used correctly and glossed on first use: **fàire** (watch) · **solas**
(light) · **tùr** (tower) · **taigh** (house) · **muir** (sea) · **oidhche**

Navigation: **An Solas** · **An Tùr** · **An Taigh** · **Na Daoine** ·

## The signature interaction — "An Solas"

The one bold spend, built and critiqued first.

The skill bans time-of-day signatures and names exactly one exception: *a
lighthouse's timed character.* This build takes that permission and spends it
on **identification and geometry — not atmosphere.** Nothing here animates the
hour; it animates a signal.

**Two halves, one panel.**

**1. An comharra — the character, running true.** The light flashes at its real
rhythm: flash 0.3 s, eclipse 2.7 s, flash 0.3 s, eclipse 16.7 s, period 20 s.
A period bar runs beneath it as a true-scale timeline with the eclipses drawn
dark and the flashes drawn in *solas*, and a seconds counter runs with it.

Then the point: **four neighbouring lights run at the same time**, each with
its real character —

| Light | Character | Why it differs |
|---|---|---|
| Rubha na Fàire | `Fl(2) W 20s` | ours |
| Rubha Reidh | `Fl(4) W 15s` | four flashes, shorter period |
| Ardnamurchan | `Fl W 20s` | same period, single flash |
| Eilean Glas | `Fl(3) W 20s` | same period, three flashes |

Watch them together and the site's best fact becomes self-evident: **at 20
seconds you cannot tell Ardnamurchan from Fàire by brightness, position or
colour — only by counting.** That is why every light in the world has a
different character, and why a mariner identifies one with a stopwatch rather
than with their eyes. Selecting a light also switches the panel to it, so you
are comparing rather than reading a table.

**2. An t-astar — how far it reaches.** A drawn section over the curve of the
earth, computed live from the real formula:

> geographic range in nautical miles = **2.08 × (√H + √h)**, H and h in metres

You set **h**, the height of your own eye, from five real positions — a kayak
(1 m), a yacht cockpit (2 m), a fishing boat wheelhouse (5 m), the cottage
window (11 m), a ship's bridge (30 m). The drawing redraws the horizon and the
distance at which the light goes under it.

The honest fact falls straight out of the arithmetic, and it is better than the
one this document originally claimed: **the published range is 24 M, and the
earth hides the light at 15.1 M from a kayak and 19.9 M even from the cottage
window.** It takes a **ship's bridge at 30 m** before the lamp, rather than the
curve of the earth, is what stops you. So the published figure is correct and
is *not about you* — and the site says so, because a house that let you assume
otherwise would be lying with a true number.

**JS off / `?nofx` / reduced motion:** the light renders **lit**, at the first
flash of its period, with the whole timeline drawn and every number printed —
a rhythmic light must never render as an unlit lamp. The range section renders
at the default eye height and its controls are real radios.

## The second interaction — "An Tùr", nine points into the instrument

Vince asked for a lot of interactive points and detail modals, and the tower
is where they honestly live. A drawn section of the tower and lantern carries
**nine numbered hotspots**, each opening a detail dialog per **commitment 10** —
not a bigger picture, but the record: the part's name in three registers (the
trade's, the Gaelic, the plain English), its numbers, and what a keeper
actually did with it at two in the morning.

1. **An lionsa** — the first-order Fresnel optic, 920 mm focal distance
2. **An amar airgid-beò** — the mercury bath the optic floats on, 250 kg
3. **An gleoc** — the clockwork and the weight tube; rewound every 30 minutes
4. **An lampa** — paraffin vapour burner, then electric in 1961
5. **Na h-astragail** — the astragals, and why they are set diagonally
6. **An t-àileadair** — the ventilator and cowl
7. **An gailearaidh** — the gallery and its handrail
8. **An seòmar-frithealaidh** — the service room below the lantern
9. **An staidhre** — 96 steps, and the worn side of them

Each dialog is a native `<dialog>`: real link fallback, focus trap and Escape
free, backdrop closes, focus returns to the pin. The house's rooms reuse the
same component.

## Video — where it earns its place

Vince asked for video in a few places, and this register is the one where the
profile's own test (*could CSS fake it?*) is most often answered **no**,
because the subject is literally a moving light.

1. **An gath** — the beam sweeping out over the sea. The hero. CSS cannot fake
   a beam in haze.
2. **An lionsa** — the optic turning in the lantern room, prisms passing.
3. **Am mullach** — the beam crossing the bedroom ceiling. This is the site's
   entire thesis in four seconds and is the reason the page exists.
4. **A' mhuir** — swell against the rocks, lit intermittently by the beam.

Each generated from the exact still it layers over, desktop-only, lazy,
`muted playsinline loop`, poster = the still, and disabled entirely under
`prefers-reduced-motion` and `?nofx`.

## Floors (binding)

Complete with JS off — **and the light renders lit**; visible solas focus
states; body contrast ≥ 7:1 measured against both grounds; no text below 11px
including SVG labels at mobile scale; full keyboard paths including Escape and
focus return from all nine dialogs; reduced-motion gets the finished static
composition and no video; every image ≤ 500KB, every video ≤ 4MB; dedicated
mobile pass at 390px.

## Never

Pure `#000` · lens flare, god-rays, star filters · storm-and-solitude romance
· "the loneliest place in Britain" · shipwreck or tragedy copy · a light that
renders dark in its static state · a second warm accent · lamp-on-void artifact
plates (that is Nocturne, and it is this register's specific failure mode) ·
tartan, bagpipes, whisky, Highland-cliché dressing · Gaelic used decoratively
or incorrectly · a card grid of rooms · a generic checkout.

## Copy voice

A station log: dated, numeric, unsentimental, and specific to the point of
dryness — which is what makes the one emotional line land when it comes.
*"Manned for a hundred and thirty-one years. Automated on the fourth of
October 1988, at ten past three in the afternoon."* *"The light does not need
you. That is the whole of what changed."* Never lonely, never wild, never
rugged.

## Build status (completed 2026-08-07, full critique loop)

**The register was the build.** Vigil is proposed here as a fifth quality of
light, with its own taste profile at `docs/vigil-taste-profile.md`. The
argument that carried it is one sentence — *Vesper's subjects are built on not
being seen; a lighthouse exists in order to be seen* — plus the observation
that Nocturne is a lamp in a room and this is a lamp in a world. The naming
family closes it: Vespers and Vigils are the two night offices.

**Imagery.** Thirteen plates on `nano_banana_pro` at 2k, one constant
art-direction phrase, mean luma 33–96 — a true night register, and every plate
generously modelled rather than underexposed. **Three faces**, as asked: Iain
in the lantern doorway, Mòrag beside the optic with her tool roll, Dòmhnall on
the jetty. The content filter refused six prompts across the batch (the lantern
interior twice, the cottage twice, Mòrag once); each was re-framed rather than
dropped, and Mòrag's replacement — inside the lantern rather than out on the
gallery — is a better picture than the brief asked for.

**Video: three shipped** (`gath` the beam sweeping, `lionsa` the optic turning,
`mullach` the beam crossing the bedroom ceiling), each generated from the exact
still it layers over and re-encoded silent at 0.29–0.69 MB. A fourth (swell on
the rocks) failed generation and was not missed. Every one passes the profile's
own test — a moving light is the one thing CSS cannot fake.

**Signature built and critiqued first, three passes:**

1. The lamp box was two-thirds empty and the count line had collapsed
   whitespace (`2flashes`). The timeline moved inside the box, making it one
   statement; `{" "}` fixed the spacing.
2. **The range drawing was geometrically wrong, and the arithmetic caught it.**
   The sight line was drawn as a *chord* to the sea curve where it must be a
   *tangent*, and the drop constant used 0.2692 (no refraction) while the range
   formula's 2.08 already contains the refraction correction. The picture
   therefore disagreed with the numbers printed beside it. Rebuilt with y
   measured downward from the eye, `sea(d) = h + k·d²`, `sight(d) = 2·k·dh·d`,
   `k = 0.2301`. **Verified: the lamp now lands on the sight line to within
   0.17 m at every eye height**, which is what "geographic range" means.
3. The tower floated in an oversized box and the astragals spilled outside the
   glazing. Box cropped to the drawing's true extent, astragals clipped, beams
   and ground line shortened to stop forcing the margins.

**The honest fact came out of the arithmetic and corrected this document.**
The original draft claimed the earth hides the light at 19.9 M from a kayak;
it is 15.1 M, and 19.9 M is the figure from the cottage window. Better still,
it takes a **ship's bridge at 30 m** before the lamp rather than the earth is
the limit — so the published 24 M is correct and is *not about you*. A fifth
eye height was added so that branch can actually be reached.

**Interactive points: thirteen records, nine on the tower and four in the
house**, each a native `<dialog>` per commitment 10 carrying the part's name in
three registers, its numbers, and what a keeper did with it at two in the
morning. All thirteen verified programmatically: every one opens with content,
Enter opens from a focused pin, focus moves inside, Escape closes, **focus
returns to the exact pin**, and `aria-expanded` toggles both ways. No JS errors.

**Floors verified:**

- **JS off — and the light renders lit.** The Vigil floor. Lamp glowing,
  timeline drawn with both flashes, all numbers printed, controls real radios.
- **Reduced motion** — emulated: 0 of 5 sections hidden, lamp held lit, 0
  videos loaded.
- **Contrast, computed** — no failures. aol 15.3:1 on muir; ceo 8.6:1;
  ceo-lo 5.7:1 and still 4.6:1 on the darkest ground; solas 11.7:1.
- **11px floor** — source clean, and the SVG trap handled twice, differently:
  the range labels are **hidden** below 860px (they would collide at any usable
  size, and the readouts carry the numbers), while the timeline's five second
  labels are **grown** to 40px (there is room, and the scale is the point).
- Detector 0 findings, no waivers; em-dash advisory cleared.
- Form walked in every state; error state screenshotted.

`npm run build` passes clean.
