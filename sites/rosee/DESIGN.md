# ROSÉE — Design source of truth

*Written 2026-07-31, before any component. A single-perfumer house in the
Aubade register: Plascassier, Pays de Grasse.*

## The one-line brief

**A 50 ml bottle is a field. We will tell you exactly how much of one.**

## Identity

**Name:** **Rosée** — French for *dew*. Jasmine and rose de mai are picked
before the sun reaches them, while the dew is still on the flower, because
that is when the oil is highest and because heat drives it off within the
hour. The name is the condition the whole craft depends on.

**Series:** **Aubade** — and this is the call worth recording, because the
backlog proposed Nocturne and the entire perfume category is photographed
dark.

The reflex is wrong here. This house's defining act is the **harvest**, and
the harvest happens between about four and nine in the morning: jasmine
grandiflorum is picked at first light and must reach extraction within a few
hours or it is worth nothing. A dark register would photograph the marketing
of perfume rather than the making of it. The subject's native hour is the
cool, clear, dew-wet Provençal dawn — the exact centre of the Aubade band.
Choosing Nocturne here would have produced the single most templated look in
luxury; choosing Aubade is what makes the site look like nobody else's.

**Place:** **Plascassier**, a hamlet of Grasse in the hills above Cannes,
where jasmine grandiflorum is still grown in walled plots on *restanques*
(dry-stone terraces). Rose de mai comes from a neighbour's field at Le
Bar-sur-Loup; everything else the house buys, and says where from.

**The nose:** **Margaux Estève**, who trained in Grasse and worked eleven
years composing for houses that never printed her name. Three perfumes, no
licensing, no flankers, no fourth until there is a reason for one.

**Proposition:** three perfumes, made from a field we can point at.

## The register argument (recorded because it was the call)

- **Native hour.** Four to nine in the morning, with dew on the flower. Not a
  choice of mood — a horticultural constraint that dictates the whole year.
- **Not a hotel.** Aubade's hospitality is replaced by the acts of the craft
  (the Veta and Nipis precedent): hands in a basket, petals in a tray, a
  perfumer at the organ. No models, no bare shoulders, no black-and-gold.
- **Distance.** The Foundry has no scent site, nothing in Provence, and
  nothing whose product is invisible. The nearest Aubade siblings are a
  surfboard bay in Oaxaca and a barong bench in Laguna.

**The mai rule**: the dusty rose is the single applied colour and appears only
where the flower would — the active material in the signature, link
underlines, focus rings, one primary action per view. Held dark and
desaturated on purpose: a bright pink would drag the site toward the beauty
aisle, which is precisely what this house is not. If it exceeds ~5% of a
frame, pull back. Note the deliberate avoidance of sage green and terracotta,
both Aubade instant-fails.

Body text is encre on pétale (measured, not eyeballed).

## Type

- **Jost** — the working voice: quantities, millilitres, percentages, prices,

## Signature geometry

**The blossom mark.** A single five-petal jasmine glyph, drawn once, used as
the section threshold, the favicon, and the counting unit in the signature —
where thousands of them are what a bottle actually costs.

## Page arc

`Hero (hands in jasmine at first light) → La cueillette (why four in the
morning) → De la fleur à l'absolue (concrète, alambic, the yields) → Ce qu'il

One line: *a field is picked before sunrise, reduced to a few grams of
absolute, composed into three perfumes — and the page will tell you exactly
how many flowers are in the bottle you are about to buy.*

## The signature interaction — "Ce qu'il y a dedans": what is actually in it

The one bold spend, built and critiqued first, fused with the order per
doctrine. **Deliberately not the scent pyramid** — top/heart/base appearing on
scroll is the most templated device in the category, and it is also passive.
And per the collection's standing warning, it is not a time-of-day device
either.

Instead: **choose a perfume and a size, and the page counts the field.**

Two controls:

1. **Le parfum** — Plascassier (jasmine-led) / Mai (rose-led) / Restanque
   (lavender and cistus, the dry hillside).
2. **Le format** — Extrait 30 ml · Eau de parfum 50 ml · Eau de parfum 100 ml
   · Recharge 100 ml.

From those, one model computes the *actual raw plant matter* behind that
bottle, from published-style yields: jasmine at roughly 5,600 blossoms per
gram of absolute, rose centifolia at about 260 flowers per gram, neroli at
3,300 blossoms, orris at 500 g of rhizome (aged three years) per gram of
butter. The readout is stated in flowers, fruits, and grams — not in
percentages.

The drawing is **a counted field**: a loose grid of the jasmine glyph where
each mark stands for a fixed number of blossoms, redrawn as the numbers
change, beside a proportional bar of the formula. Choosing the 100 ml fills
the frame; the recharge visibly costs the same field but not the bottle.

It also states, without being asked, **which materials are synthetic** and
what proportion of the formula they are — because the honest answer for the
musks and woods is "made in a laboratory, and here is why that is better."

**"Ajouter à la commande"** adds the chosen bottle to the order below.

**JS off / `?nofx` / reduced motion:** renders the default (Plascassier, 50
ml) fully drawn with the whole readout printed, both controls as real radios,
and every price listed in the order table — so the form still submits. The
reveal animation is gated behind `html.js` set by an inline head script.

## How the site takes an order — a real cart

This is the most genuinely retail of the three sites in this batch, and the
order section behaves accordingly: **a priced line for every product, a
quantity stepper on each, and a running subtotal.**

| Product | Price |
|---|---:|
| Extrait, 30 ml | €185 |
| Eau de parfum, 50 ml | €140 |
| Eau de parfum, 100 ml | €210 |
| Recharge, 100 ml (your bottle stays) | €150 |
| Coffret de découverte, 5 × 2 ml | €28 |

The *coffret* is refundable against a first full bottle, which is stated
plainly. Subtotal, shipping, and contact are collected, and the whole order
hands off by **mailto** with `enctype="text/plain"` as a legible order
request — never a card, never a real payment. With JS off the steppers are
native number inputs, every unit price is printed, and the order still sends;
only the live subtotal needs script.

## Copy voice

Quantitative and unmystical. Blossom counts, grams, yields, hours between
picking and extraction, which materials came from a field and which from a
laboratory. Never "notes that seduce", never "olfactory journey", never an
origin myth. The romance is allowed to come from the true numbers, which are
already absurd.

## Imagery — the Rosée recipe

**One recipe: cool clear Provençal dawn just before sunrise, dew on
everything, long low light.** Bright Aubade band. Rose appears only as real
petals. People are the pickers' hands and the nose at her organ — never a
model, never a bare shoulder, never a bottle on black.

Constant batch phrase: *"clear cool Provençal dawn light just before sunrise,
dew still on everything, pale limestone and wicker and glass, soft generous
natural light with long low shadows, documentary craft photography, shallow
depth of field, no text, no logos."*

Shot list: `hero-cueillette`, `champ`, `jasmin`, `rose-mai`, `alambic`,
`concrete`, `orgue`, `nez` (human presence), `collection`, `commander`.

## Floors (binding)

Complete with JS off (order submits, signature drawn, prices printed);
visible mai focus states; body contrast ≥ 7:1 **measured**; full keyboard

mobile pass at 390px.

## Never

Black-and-gold perfume advertising · a bottle on a dark reflective surface ·
models, skin, or bare shoulders · "olfactory journey" / "seduction" copy ·
the scroll-driven scent pyramid · sage green or terracotta · rounded floating
cards · a card-capture checkout · mystifying the synthetics instead of naming
them.

## Build status (completed 2026-08-01, full critique loop)

All 10 planned finals generated plus a Restanque hillside plate (11 total); no
CSS placeholders needed. Optimized to `public/images/` as full (≤1536px) plus
`-800` variants, every file ≤500KB (largest 470KB).

Measured mean luma: jasmin 154, commander 131, nez 131, champ 124, collection
119, concrete 107, orgue 107, rose-mai 103, alambic 101, restanque 98,
hero-cueillette 78. The optimizer's flag was retuned to 65 for this site: the
subject's hour is *before sunrise*, genuinely dimmer than Nipis's backlit
window, so the inherited threshold of 90 would have condemned a correct dawn
plate. Human presence: the picker's hands in the basket, and Margaux at the
organ.

Signature "Ce qu'il y a dedans" built and critiqued first (3 passes):

1. The counting mark rendered as a teardrop blob rather than jasmine. Rebuilt
   as a real five-petal glyph defined once in `<defs>` and instanced with
   `<use>` — which also matters at 373 copies for the largest bottle.
2. The frame's empty upper half read as dead space rather than as headroom.
   Added a ghosted layer drawing the frame's *full capacity* at 8% opacity, so
   the fill level reads as a measure. This is what makes the three perfumes
   comparable at a glance.
3. Scale tuning: one mark = 25 flower heads, fixed across all perfumes on
   purpose. Verified interactively (`tools/shot-el.mjs`) at 100 ml: Plascassier
   nearly fills the frame (373 marks), Mai fills about three rows (82),
   Restanque about one and a half (32).

Because Restanque is measured in grams of hillside rather than flower heads, a
flower count alone would have misrepresented it. The model therefore also
computes **total raw plant matter**, which is the headline and which compares
all three honestly: 1.26 kg, 933 g, and 378 g per 50 ml.

The order is the most genuinely retail of the three sites in this batch: five
priced lines with quantity steppers and a live subtotal, handing off by mailto
as an order request. "Ajouter à la commande" fills the matching row and
scrolls to it.
