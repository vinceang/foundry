# TRACCIA — Design source of truth

*Written 2026-08-06, before any component. A custom steel frame builder in Val
Camonica, Lombardy, in the Pastorale register: cold flat light, bare steel, and
a road that writes the specification.*

## The one-line brief

**A frame is a set of angles argued from one rider and one road.**

## Identity

**Name:** **Traccia** — Italian for a traced line and also for a route. Both
meanings are the work: the builder *traccia* the geometry full size on the
board before a tube is cut, and the buyer's *traccia* is the road they intend
to ride. The name is one word doing the whole job of the brand, and it is
ordinary Italian rather than invented.

**Series:** **Pastorale**, chosen by Vince against the backlog's Nocturne. The
call is worth recording because the obvious objection is that framebuilding is
an indoor craft with a torch in it, which sounds exactly like Nocturne.

Three arguments, in order of weight:

1. **The collection already holds three Italian Nocturnes**, and they are all
   warm: Sant'Agata (Cremona, varnish and wood), Barchetta (Naples, a lit board
   in a dark room), Alla Luna (Murano, furnace heat). A fourth warm Italian
   workshop is precisely the failure the relentless test exists to catch.
   **A cold, bright, alpine Italian site is genuinely new to the collection**,
   and that alone justifies the register.
2. **The light in this trade really is cold and flat.** You cannot read a mitre
   gap, a brazing fillet or a 0.3 mm alignment error under a warm point source.
   Frame shops work to tall north windows and daylight-balanced light for the
   same reason a drawing office does. The lamp-on-void plate would be a lie
   about how this work is actually seen.
3. **The frame's specification comes from outdoors.** What decides the angles is
   a rider's body and a road's gradient, surface and length — and those live on
   the Mortirolo in February, not at the bench. The bench executes; the valley
   specifies. That is a Pastorale thesis, not a Nocturne one.

**Not Tyri.** The register's only sibling is a Numedal log yard. The distance is
material and structural: cold *steel* rather than warm timber, a **red-oxide**
warm note rather than resin amber, a **cold** near-black (mill scale) rather
than Tyri's warm pine tar, Italian rather than Norwegian, and a signature that
argues angles rather than assembling a joint.

**Place:** A shop in **Edolo, high Val Camonica** (Brescia, Lombardy) — a valley
that has worked iron since the Camunni, sitting under the **Mortirolo** and the
**Gavia**. Two of the hardest paved climbs in Europe start within an hour of the
door, and the *strade bianche* of the Valle delle Messi run off the same road.
A builder here does not have to imagine the test conditions.

**Founded:** 1978, in a shed behind a bicycle repair shop. Second generation.

**The maker:** one builder, one bench. He cuts, mitres, brazes, files and aligns
every frame himself, and paints none of them — painting goes down the valley to
Brescia, and he says so.

**Proposition:** a frame drawn for one body and one road.

## Money, and therefore the order form

A frameset — frame, fork, headset cups faced and fitted — runs **€2,850–€4,150**.
Under the $5,000 line, so commitment 7 makes a **real structured order form the
primary path**. It takes fit numbers and a road, hands off an order request by
mailto, and never touches a card. Complete bicycles are quoted separately and
said to be, because the build kit is not this house's craft.

**The minio rule** (this register's version of Tyri's amber): *minio* is the
orange-red oxide primer a bare steel frame is sprayed with before it is painted
— the only colour that exists in this shop before the frame leaves it. It
appears only where the builder has actually marked something: the scribed line
in the signature, the active state, focus rings, one primary action per view.
Over ~5% of a frame it is paint, not primer; pull back.

Note the two deliberate inversions of Tyri: **minio `#a8452a` is a red-orange
oxide where Tyri's `#a8701f` is a golden brown**, and **calamina is a *cold*
near-black where Tyri's pine tar is warm**. Same register, different metal.

Body text is calamina on bruma (verify ≥ 7:1 by measurement, not eye).

## Type

- **Bodoni Moda** — the display voice. Bodoni was cut in Parma; its hairlines
  are as thin as a drawn line and as thin as a butted tube wall, which is the
  right coincidence for this subject. Regular weight, generous size, restrained
  — the danger is fashion-magazine, and the defence is discipline.
- **Titillium Web** — the working voice. Designed at the Accademia di Belle
  Arti di Urbino, squared and technical, with numerals that hold a column.
  Angles, millimetres, tolerances, the order form.

Both are genuinely Italian, and neither appears on a sibling (Tyri:
Besley/Schibsted; Sant'Agata, Barchetta, Alla Luna all different again).

## Signature geometry

**The lug point.** A hand-cut lug's tapering spearpoint — the one piece of
ornament in framebuilding, filed by hand, and the detail by which one builder
is told from another. It is the site's only ornament: chapter marks, the
favicon, the checked state, list marks. Everything else is drawn as a line,
because everything else here *is* a line. Zero border-radius.

## The opening move — the dimension and its tolerance

**Not** a tracked caps label, and not Tyri's ring count. Each chapter opens as
**a line off the build sheet**: a fine dimension rule with the quantity, its
value, and the tolerance it is held to, set in the working face.

| Chapter | The measure | Why it is that chapter's |
|---|---|---|
| La valle | `PENDENZA · 10,5 % media / 18 % max` | the Mortirolo, which sets the gearing and the geometry |
| L'acciaio | `SPESSORE · 0,7 – 0,4 – 0,7 mm` | butted wall thickness — the whole argument for steel |
| La traccia | `ANGOLO STERZO · 71°30′ – 73°30′` | the range the signature moves through |
| La dima | `ALLINEAMENTO · ± 0,3 mm sul piano` | the tolerance the shop actually holds |
| Le mani | `ORE · 34 al telaio` | hours in one frame |
| La commissione | `ATTESA · 14 settimane` | the wait, stated before it is asked for |

It earns its place because a tolerance is the most honest thing a maker can
publish, and because each number is used by the chapter beneath it. Recorded
here so it holds site-wide and cannot drift into decoration.

## The signature interaction — "La traccia"

The one bold spend, built and critiqued first, fused with the order form per
doctrine. **A large scale side elevation of the frame, drawn live from the
buyer's body and the road they name.** It is not Veta's outline: this argues
*angles*, and it has a conflict in it that cannot be designed away.

**Inputs — the five things a builder actually asks first:**

1. **Altezza** — height, cm.
2. **Cavallo** — inseam, cm. Height and inseam together, not height alone,
   because the same 178 cm rider can need two different seat tubes.
3. **Allungo** — reach: *raccolto* / *neutro* / *disteso*.
4. **La strada** — **the road, which is an input and not a preference:**
   - **Passo** — alpine tarmac, long climbs and fast descents. 28 mm.
   - **Bianca** — *strade bianche*, gravel and broken surfaces. 38 mm.
   - **Randonnée** — long distance, loaded, front bag. 32 mm.
5. **Peso del ciclista** — rider weight, which chooses the tube gauges.

**Outputs — all real quantities, all computed from one model:**

- **Angolo sterzo** and **angolo piantone** (head and seat angles)
- **Passo** (wheelbase, mm) and **avancorsa** (trail, mm), trail computed
  properly: `trail = (R·cos θ − offset) / sin θ`
- **Stack** and **reach** (mm)
- **Tubi** — the tube set and wall thicknesses picked for the rider's weight
- **Peso del telaio** (g) and the price

The drawing redraws entirely on every change: seat tube, top tube, head tube,
stays, fork rake, and the two tyre circles at their true clearance.

**The conflict — sovrapposizione (toe overlap).** On a short frame with a big
tyre the front wheel meets the rider's toe on a slow turn. It is real, it is
common, and there is no geometry that removes it without costing something
else. The drawing shows the toe arc crossing the tyre, and the house states its
position and the three answers honestly: slacken the head angle and lengthen
the front-centre (which slows the steering), fit a narrower tyre (which loses
the road you chose it for), or accept it (which most riders do, and which is
what the builder himself rides). **The product argues with the buyer**, and the
argument is drawn to scale.

**«Comincia questo telaio»** carries the whole specification into the order
form and scrolls to it.

**JS off / `?nofx` / reduced motion:** the SVG renders a sensible default frame
fully drawn with every quantity printed, and the controls are real inputs
inside the form, so the order still submits the chosen spec. All reveal
animation is gated behind an `html.js` class set by an inline head script.

## How the site takes an order

**La commissione** collects: the five signature inputs (pre-filled), the
finish (bare *minio* primer, single colour, or the buyer's own painter),
whether the fork is to match, the dropout spacing, name, email, and the town
they ride from. Mailto handoff with `enctype="text/plain"`. Every state
designed — rest, focus, filled, error (named beside the field in the trade's
plain words), submitting, and a **docket** confirmation stating what was
ordered, the wait in weeks, and what happens next.

## Imagery — the Traccia recipe

**One recipe: cold flat overcast light, bare steel, and one oxide-red note.**
Pastorale is a *bright* register — target mean luma 110–170 — and the failure
to guard against is a murky plate, never a bright one. No lamp, no chiaroscuro,
no golden hour. Indoor plates are lit by tall north windows, which is how this
work is genuinely seen; the brazing flame is the single warm point in a cold
room and is allowed to be exactly that.

Constant batch phrase: *"cold overcast alpine daylight, flat even light with no
sun, pale grey-white sky, old snow and bare rock, cold blue-grey shadows, bare
steel and one oxide-red note as the only warmth, documentary craft photography,
muted cold palette, rich and fully modelled rather than murky, no text, no
lettering, no logos, no watermark."*

**Every bicycle plate must be prompted unbranded and undecalled** — generators
put invented brand names on bike frames at the slightest opportunity, and a
decal is a hard reject.

Shot list: `passo` (hero — a frame at a pass marker), `strada` (white gravel
switchbacks), `tubi` (the tube bundle, butted section), `dima` (the frame in
the jig), `brasatura` (**human** — hands brazing a lug), `congiunzione` (macro,
the hand-cut lug point), `riscontro` (the alignment table and dial gauge),
`mani` (**human** — the builder sighting down a frame), `minio` (frames hanging
in primer), `ghiaia` (the gravel surface, textural), `tavolo` (the full-size
geometry drawn on the board), `valle` (a finished frame in the valley).

## Copy voice

A builder's, not a brand's: numbers, tolerances, gradients, hours, and what he
will not do. *"The road decides more of this frame than you do."* *"Thirty-four
hours, and eleven of them are filing."* *"He does not paint them. Painting is a
trade and it is not his."* The one formal admission, stated where a buyer will
see it: **most riders his size ride with toe overlap, including him.**


## Build status (completed 2026-08-06, full critique loop)

**The register argument held up in practice.** Set beside Sant'Agata, Barchetta
and Alla Luna, this reads as a different country: cold flat light on bare
steel, a near-white ground, and a red-oxide accent where the siblings all run
warm. The Pastorale call Vince made against the backlog's Nocturne is the
reason the site is distinguishable at all.

**Imagery.** Twelve plates on `nano_banana_pro` at 2k (Higgsfield), one
constant art-direction phrase. Measured mean luma 88–168, correctly in
Pastorale's bright band — the Nocturne 20–40 band does not apply here and the
failure to guard against is a murky plate. The `strada` shot (gravel
switchbacks) was refused by the content filter three times and was dropped;
`valle` and `ghiaia` carry that chapter instead. **Every bicycle prompt was
written unbranded** — generators put invented decals on frames at the
slightest opening, and none of the shipped plates has one.

Bright detailed plates compress far worse than a Nocturne's dark ground: at
q78 the largest was **717KB**, over the floor. `optimize.sh` now steps quality
down per file until each clears 500KB (largest shipped: 485KB).

**Signature built and critiqued first, three passes:**

1. Labels were written across the tubes, the trail dimension sat under the
   front wheel, and the toe callout — the point of the whole drawing — was the
   faintest thing on it. Dimensions moved below the ground line or into the
   empty main triangle; nothing is now written across a tube.
2. The wheels were drawn as dark as the frame and competed with it; lightened
   to a middle grey so the frame reads as the subject. A seat-post stub was
   added so the frame stops looking severed.
3. The toe callout still collided with the front wheel; moved into the clear
   space above its own bar, and the sub-label dropped.

**The geometry is real arithmetic, and it was checked against the world before
anything was drawn on top of it.** A 178/84 rider on *passo* gets head angle
72°48′, trail 57 mm, wheelbase 998 mm, front centre 595 mm — textbook road
numbers. *Randonnée* produces **44 mm trail** from a 65 mm fork offset, which
is genuine low-trail practice for a front load. *Bianca* produces 65 mm, which
is genuinely gravel-stable. One sign error was caught this way: the contact
patch trails *behind* where the steering axis meets the ground, and taking it
the other way put the wheelbase 110 mm long.

**The conflict is real and unavoidable**, which is what makes it worth
building: a 156/70 rider overlaps by ~50 mm on any road, a 192/94 rider is
clear, and the three answers each cost something. Verified across all three
states — clear hides the answers panel, overlap turns the toe arc and the bar
minio.

**Floors verified, not assumed:**

- **JS off** — page complete, frame fully drawn, all six readouts printed,
  sliders and radios real, and the order form still submits the spec.
- **Reduced motion** — emulated: 0 of 6 `.rise` sections hidden, SVG
  transitions `0s`, all seven tubes drawn.
- **Contrast, computed.** Two real failures. `--acciaio-lo` was **3.82:1** and
  failed 4.5:1 outright; `--acciaio` was 6.01:1 and missed Foundry's 7:1 body
  floor. Solved against *bruma*, the darker of the two text grounds, to
  `#586366` (5.21:1) and `#414d51` (7.35:1). Calamina on bruma 15.1:1.
- **11px floor** — source clean, and the SVG trap caught again: at 390px this
  board renders at 0.29×, where **no** font size clears 11px without the labels
  colliding. Fixed properly rather than by inflating type: the readout grew from
  four cells to **six** (adding seat/top tube and stack/reach), so every number
  the drawing carries is in HTML, and the SVG labels are dropped below 860px.
  The drawing becomes a drawing.
- Detector 0 findings, no waivers. Em-dash advisory cleared by varying the
  cadence in eight places.
- Form walked in every state; error state screenshotted.

`npm run build` passes clean.
